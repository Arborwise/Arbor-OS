New neighborhoods can create old-fashioned tree problems very quickly.

Planting too deep, buried trunk flares, compacted soil, drainage changes, damaged roots, narrow lots, and early structural defects can follow a tree for years.

Planting and root-zone problems

Buried flares, excessive mulch, girdling roots, compacted soil, and poor drainage can appear later as canopy stress.

⌂

Clearance on tighter lots

Rooflines, fences, driveways, sidewalks, and neighboring properties need selective pruning with a clear objective.

⚡

Wind and storm exposure

Newly exposed trees, weak unions, broken limbs, and recent movement deserve prompt evaluation and controlled work.

Start below the canopy

Brown leaves may be the last part of the story, not the first.

Canopy symptoms can begin with root cutting, compaction, irrigation changes, poor drainage, drought, planting depth, insects, disease, or several stresses together.

  • Confirm whether the trunk flare is visible.
  • Keep mulch and soil away from the trunk.
  • Consider recent grading, trenching, fencing, pools, patios, or utility work.
  • Compare the pattern across the whole canopy.
  • Separate health decline from immediate structural risk.

Annie’s tip: A new tree is not automatically a correctly planted tree. Check the trunk flare before the canopy tells you something is wrong.
