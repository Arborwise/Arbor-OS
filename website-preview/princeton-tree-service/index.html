Arborwise looks beyond the leaves to understand whether the concern involves roots, soil, water, construction, structure, pests, disease, weather, or several factors together.

Drainage and watering

Planting and structure

Buried trunk flares, girdling roots, poor staking, competing leaders, and weak branch attachments can become bigger problems as a tree grows.

Read the pattern

One brown branch and a thinning whole canopy tell different stories.

The location and pattern of symptoms help narrow what Arborwise inspects next. A website can teach the clues, but an on-site review is needed to understand the exact tree and property.

  • Photograph the full canopy from more than one side.
  • Show the trunk base and surrounding soil.
  • Tell us about recent construction, trenching, irrigation, storms, or grade changes.
  • Note whether the change was sudden or gradual.
  • Keep people away if the tree or soil is actively moving.

Annie’s tip: Do not diagnose a whole tree from one leaf. Show Arborwise the pattern, the base, and what changed around it.

The Arborwise decision process

Understand the cause before choosing the solution.

Pruning cannot fix every root problem. Treatment cannot correct every structural defect. Removal is not the answer to every symptom. Arborwise matches the recommendation to the actual concern.

Prune with an objective

Structure, deadwood, clearance, balance, or selective reduction, with correct cuts and no topping.

Correct the site when possible

Root flare, mulch, watering, soil, drainage, and protection from further disturbance may be part of the plan.

Remove when retention is unreasonable

Dead, failing, severely damaged, structurally compromised, or unreasonably risky trees need controlled removal planning.

Fast answers

Princeton tree questions

Can construction or trenching damage a tree?

Yes. Root cutting, compaction, grade changes, and drainage changes can affect both tree health and stability. The visible response may be immediate or delayed.

Does a thinning canopy mean the tree should be removed?

Not automatically. Arborwise looks at the pattern, roots, soil, trunk, structure, recent changes, and nearby targets before recommending pruning, treatment, monitoring, or removal.

Can Arborwise help with young trees?

Yes. Arborwise can review planting depth, trunk flare, staking, root-zone conditions, structure, and early pruning needs.

Does Arborwise handle storm damage in Princeton?

Yes. Arborwise provides priority storm-damage response and helps identify immediate hazards and the next sensible step.
