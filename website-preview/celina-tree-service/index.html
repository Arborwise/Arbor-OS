Construction and clearance

Grade changes, trenching, root cutting, hardscape, roofs, fences, and driveways can change what a tree can tolerate.

Look below the leaves

A thin canopy does not always begin in the canopy.

The cause may be roots, soil, drainage, watering, planting depth, weather, insects, disease, or several factors together.

  • Check whether the trunk flare is visible.
  • Look for soil or mulch piled against the trunk.
  • Consider recent trenching, grading, construction, or irrigation changes.
  • Compare the whole canopy pattern, not one leaf.
  • Separate urgent movement from slower health decline.

Annie’s tip: The root zone is much wider than the trunk. Protecting the soil protects the tree.

The Arborwise approach

Do the least harmful work that solves the real problem.

Tree care should improve structure, reduce a real risk, correct a site problem, or support health. Cutting without a clear objective can create the next problem.

Prune early, prune selectively

Smaller, well-placed cuts can prevent larger structural corrections later.

Protect roots and soil

Compaction, trenching, grade changes, poor drainage, and buried flares can affect the entire canopy.

Remove only when appropriate

When a tree is dead, failing, badly compromised, or unreasonably risky, Arborwise plans the removal carefully.

Celina tree questions

Can Arborwise help with newly planted trees?

Yes. Arborwise can look at planting depth, trunk flare, staking, root-zone conditions, structure, watering, and early pruning needs.

Do brown leaves always mean disease?

No. Heat, drought, watering, drainage, roots, soil, insects, disease, and seasonal change can produce similar symptoms.

Can construction damage show up later?

Yes. Root cutting, compaction, grade changes, trenching, and drainage changes may affect a canopy after the disturbance.

Does Arborwise handle mature trees and removals too?

Yes. Arborwise provides pruning, condition reviews, technical removals, and priority storm-damage response for young and mature trees.
